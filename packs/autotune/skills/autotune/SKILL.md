# autotune

## Overview

Machine prose is rarely wrong. It is autotuned. Every note sits dead centre of
its pitch, and that is exactly what gives it away. A model picking the likely
next word often enough leaves the same artefacts every time: the same
connectives, the same rhythm, the same tidy triples, the same reach for the em
dash.

You are hunting statistical habits, not weak writing. Weak writing is a separate
job with separate fixes, and mixing the two means doing neither well.

**Overcorrecting fails too.** Leave the artefacts in and the piece reads as
machine work. Scrub every one of them out and it still does, because flat,
opinion-free prose is itself the loudest tell. A voice is partly the notes it
misses on purpose. Knowing when to stop is most of this skill.

## First, read the house

Before changing anything, look at what the author already does. Open the nearest few
documents: other posts on the blog, the last few commits.

Anything the house does consistently is a voice, not a tell. A writer who uses
em dashes in every piece has a convention, and stripping them is not unslopping.
It is overwriting a person.

Only what the house does *not* otherwise do counts. Skip this step and you will
edit the author into sounding like everyone else, which is the thing you were
brought in to prevent.

## Start with these

Fix this group before reading the next section. It carries most of the signal.

1. **Em dashes.** Unless the house uses them. End the sentence, or use a comma.
   Reaching for parentheses instead trades one tell for another.
2. **The triple.** Three examples, three adjectives, three clauses, when the
   real number was two or five. Count the items you actually have and use that.
3. **"Not just X, but Y."** Also "It's not about X, it's about Y" and "X isn't
   just Y, it's Z." State the point once.
4. **The summarising close.** A final paragraph restating what was already said.
   Cut it. Writing ends when the point lands, not when the shape feels complete.
5. **Even rhythm.** Sentence lengths clustered between fifteen and twenty-five
   words with nothing short. Write the lengths out as a list of numbers and look
   at them. Then break one in half.
6. **Fluent nothing.** Sentences that would sit unchanged in a competitor's
   docs. Test each by restating it as an instruction, a fact, or a number. If it
   cannot be restated, it was never saying anything.

## Then the rest

| Tell | Fix |
|---|---|
| delve, crucial, tapestry, testament, underscore, landscape, robust, seamless | The plain word |
| "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features" | "is" or "has" |
| "highlighting...", "ensuring...", "reflecting..." tacked onto a clause | Cut the clause or make it a sentence |
| Colons as mid-sentence connectors | A full stop. Colons belong before a list |
| Bold on every proper noun | Bold nothing, or bold one thing |
| "**Label:** label restated..." | Prose, or a real detail after the label |
| Title Case Headings | Sentence case |
| Curly quotes, decorative emoji | Straight quotes, no emoji |
| "In order to", "It is important to note that" | Delete |
| "Experts believe", "studies suggest" | Name the source or cut the claim |

## The stopping rule

After the pass, read the result and answer one question: **whose is this?**

If the answer is "someone careful" rather than a named person, you went too far.
Put back something only the author would write: a sentence that runs long
because they were enjoying themselves, or a word they overuse. Voice is the
habits that survive editing.

A piece can carry three or four tells and still read as human. A piece with none
and no personality reads as a press release.

## Output contract

Return, in this order:

1. **The tells found.** A table: the phrase, which tell it is, and whether the
   house does it elsewhere. This is the part that teaches. Write it before you
   edit, or you will justify whatever you already changed.
2. **The rewrite.** Same length or shorter.
3. **The count.** Tells removed, tells kept on purpose, and why.

## Leave alone

- Quotes, testimonials, anything in someone else's voice
- Technical terms of art where the reader is technical
- Any pattern the house uses consistently, however much you dislike it
- The argument. If the piece is wrong, say so separately instead of editing the
  wrongness out

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Running it without reading the house first | You cannot tell a habit from a fingerprint without a baseline. Every judgement after that is a guess. |
| Treating the list as a checklist to zero | Zero tells and zero voice is the failure this skill exists to avoid. |
| Fixing prose quality at the same time | Passive voice and long sentences are not AI tells. Humans write them constantly. Separate pass. |
| Swapping a flagged word for a fancier one | "Delve" to "explore" is lateral. "Delve into" to "read" is the fix. |
| Rewriting first, listing second | The list stops being evidence and becomes an alibi. |
